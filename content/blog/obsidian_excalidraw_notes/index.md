I take many of my academic notes by hand. Diagrams, equations, and quick annotations are often easier to draw than type, but handwritten pages are hard to search and connect to related material later.

<!--more-->

I use **Obsidian**, a notes app, with **Excalidraw**, a drawing tool that lets me write and sketch inside it. That keeps my handwritten work alongside lecture slides, textbooks, and other course materials. Links between notes help me follow related ideas, while the search tools I'm building help me find relevant passages across the collection.

OneNote and GoodNotes made writing feel natural, but their file-type limits got in the way of my processing workflow. Excalidraw is more flexible for my notes, though it is not a one-click solution: I still export drawings as SVG files before the handwriting transcription process can use them.

The setup also has practical tradeoffs. I adjusted the tablet interface to make common pen tools easier to reach and moved controls away from the operating-system gesture area. I keep large reference files out of the notes sync so the tablet only needs to download the working notes and drawings. Some plugin settings and customizations still need to be copied or enabled by hand.

Once exported, the handwritten notes can be transcribed and searched alongside the other material. A lecture can leave me with two versions of a drawing: the notes I wrote in class, and a later copy with the lecture slides pasted beside them. The search tools can now recognize some of those pairs and avoid showing both copies when the more complete version is available. Both originals remain editable.

That connection is what makes the setup useful to me. I can keep writing by hand while making the notes easier to find and revisit later. The export step and occasional sync maintenance are still part of the work.
