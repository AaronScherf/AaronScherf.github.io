# From USAID to Columbia: Sharing My Research and the Tools Behind It

*A new website for research on disasters, conflict, and recovery, alongside practical software others can use.*

This fall, I started a PhD in Sustainable Development at Columbia University. I've also launched [my personal website](https://aaronscherf.github.io/), where I'll share the research questions I'm pursuing, the software I'm building, and what I learn along the way.

Before Columbia, I worked as a Foreign Service Officer with USAID, including on program design and social inclusion in Colombia and monitoring and evaluation in wartime Ukraine. Those experiences shaped the questions I want to study: how to identify needs, direct support, and assess results when conditions change quickly and reliable information is hard to obtain.

My research will focus on how communities prepare for and recover from overlapping climate disasters, armed conflict, and economic shocks. I'm interested in the decisions that help people protect their livelihoods, avoid displacement, and rebuild over the longer term.

One question is whether approaches used in natural disaster response can be adapted to conflict settings. That includes index insurance, with payouts linked to measurable conditions such as rainfall, and cash assistance delivered before a crisis escalates. I want to understand when these tools help, how conflict changes their effectiveness, and which local conditions explain differences in outcomes.

My research plans include studies of Somalia and Ukraine, bringing together satellite observations, conflict records, market prices, and displacement data. I'll draw on economics, climate science, and machine learning, alongside my experience implementing development programs. The aim is to produce evidence that can help practitioners plan and evaluate humanitarian and social protection programs in uncertain environments.

The website connects that future work with projects I've already undertaken. My [wheat-yield research](https://aaronscherf.github.io/projects/wheat_yield_prediction/) compared satellite observations, experimental data, and prediction models. More recently, I've been building [Academic Hub](https://aaronscherf.github.io/projects/academic-hub-overview/) and an [AI Research Assistant](https://aaronscherf.github.io/projects/ai-research-assistant-overview/): Python tools for making notes and papers searchable, finding relevant literature, and developing explanations with references back to their sources.

I'll post regularly as the work develops, sharing research updates, software projects, and practical explanations of methods. Some posts will explore a substantive question about disasters or recovery; others will walk through a tool or a lesson from building it. My hope is that development practitioners, researchers, and students will each find something they can use.

Making the work reusable is part of the plan. I'll share open-source code, documentation, and examples, with clear information about the data and setup needed to reproduce an analysis or adapt a tool. Where source data cannot be redistributed, I'll explain the requirements and provide public or sample inputs where possible.

Even the [website's source and setup](https://github.com/AaronScherf/AaronScherf.github.io) are available for others to adapt. It builds on the open-source HugoBlox Academic CV template, so you can use the same foundation for your own research or project portfolio.

I'd welcome conversations with researchers working on related questions and organizations looking for help with program evaluation, data analysis, or research tools. To my former USAID colleagues and partners, I also hope this offers an easy way to stay in touch and exchange ideas as our work takes us in different directions.

Please take a look around, try a project, or get in touch. I'd especially like to hear which questions or tools would be useful in your work.
